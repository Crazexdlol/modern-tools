## SECTION B — NEW Q&As ADDED (20 questions)

**Q41–Q60** have been added to STORAGE_FAQ_v2.md covering:

### First-Party vs. Third-Party Storage Claims (Q41–Q47)
- **Q41:** Differences between first-party and third-party claim types and how they affect storage liability
- **Q42:** Processing speed, deductibles, and documentation requirements differ between claim types
- **Q43:** Insight into third-party disputes: documentation, rate benchmarking, and typical settlements (30–50% reductions)
- **Q44:** The "5-day rule" myth: no universal grace period; state variation (WA 5 days, NJ 30 days, TX variable)
- **Q45:** Liability if claimant didn't authorize the hold (answer: typically no, but claimant becomes liable for delays after notification)
- **Q46:** Multi-vehicle/multi-claim responsibility (collision vs. liability vs. UM/UIM subrogation)
- **Q47:** Can facility charge owner directly if insurers dispute? (Yes legally, but rare in practice; escalate to claims managers)

### Mechanic's Lien vs. Storage Lien (Q48–Q50)
- **Q48:** Core distinctions: storage lien is possessory only; mechanic's lien includes repairs, parts, supplies, and may allow deficiency judgments
- **Q49:** Enforcement differences: body shops can pursue court foreclosure and deficiency judgments; tow yards are limited to possessory sale
- **Q50:** What items are included in a mechanic's lien (repairs, towing, storage, diagnostics, admin fees) vs. what are not (pre-existing damage)

### State-by-State Storage Lien Specifics (Q51–Q56)
- **Q51:** Texas (5-day notice in-state, 14-day out-of-state, no notice in first 24 hours, 30+ day sale timeline)
- **Q52:** California (24-hour post notice, 48–72 hour mail notice, 30-day hold, DMV approval required for sale, no statewide rate cap)
- **Q53:** Florida (7-day notice, 30+ day sale timeline, no statewide rate cap, local ordinances control)
- **Q54:** Illinois (2-day notification to lienholder required, strict: 2-day failure voids entire lien, 30-day hold minimum)
- **Q55:** Colorado (30-day default before enforcement, 60-day minimum for vehicles, multiple notice opportunities, long hold periods)
- **Q56:** North Carolina (DMV is intermediary for notices, owner has 10 days to request judicial hearing, 30-day private sale notice / 20-day public sale notice)

### Grace Periods & Insurance Coverage (Q57–Q60)
- **Q57:** No universal grace period (WA 5 days, NJ 30 days, TX variable; best practice 3–5 business days)
- **Q58:** Can insurer charge owner for insurer-caused delays? (No; insurer absorbs delays caused by its own slow decisions)
- **Q59:** What happens to storage if claim initially denied, then accepted? (Insurer typically pays all storage back to loss date)
- **Q60:** Rental reimbursement vs. storage charges truly separate? (Yes, but verify exact policy language for combined "transportation" limits)

## Key Findings & Recommendations

### Critical Corrections:
1. **California has NO statewide rate cap** — rates are locally set. Update Q34 and any guidance tied to statewide caps.
2. **Texas statute is Occ. Code Chapter 2303, not Transp. Code § 2308.308** — update citations in all references.
3. **New York 2025-2026 proposed increases** ($25→$27 day 4+) are pending; current law still reflects older rates.
4. **Florida and Illinois have no statewide rate caps** — rates are set locally or by "reasonable" standard.
5. **No universal "5-day grace period"** — state-by-state variation requires case-by-case verification.

### New Coverage Gaps Filled:
- **First-party vs. third-party claims** — now covered comprehensively with processing differences, burden of proof, and typical dispute outcomes
- **Mechanic's lien mechanics** — distinguished from storage-only liens; deficiency judgments now explained
- **State-by-state lien procedures** — Texas, California, Florida, Illinois, Colorado, North Carolina all detailed with statutory references
- **Grace period myth** — debunked with actual state standards

### Audit & Compliance Recommendations:
- Always verify local rate caps before accepting any storage invoice; do not assume statewide caps exist
- In third-party claims, require detailed claimant timeline showing notification date, pickup availability date, and actual retrieval date
- Flag any Texas notice sent within 24 hours of intake (violates statute)
- Flag any sale conducted before statutory holding period (varies by state: 30 days typical minimum)
- Verify Illinois notifications were sent within 2 business days; non-compliance voids the entire lien
- In first-party claims, verify insurer did not cause decision delays; if so, absorb those storage days
